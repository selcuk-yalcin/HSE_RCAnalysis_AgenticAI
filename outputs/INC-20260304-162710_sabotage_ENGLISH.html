3. ANALİZ YÖNTEMİ - 5 WHY
3.1 5-Why Tekniği
The 5-Why technique was rigorously applied to each causal branch by repeatedly asking 'Why did this occur?' until reaching an organisational root cause that could not be further decomposed without moving outside the organisation's sphere of control. For example, in Branch 2 (access control failure), the chain progressed from 'Why was Walsh's card active?' (immediate: card not deactivated) through 'Why was it not deactivated?' (HR sent email but did not follow up; security did not action) and 'Why was there no follow-up?' (no formal leavers' checklist or sign-off mechanism) to ultimately 'Why was no checklist established?' (no periodic audit of security-critical processes; last security review 26 months prior). This iterative questioning revealed that the root cause was not a single procedural lapse but a systemic lack of accountability and oversight. The technique was particularly effective in distinguishing between proximate causes (e.g., broken lock, offline CCTV) and the underlying organisational decisions (e.g., treating security-critical maintenance as low-priority, tolerating known deviations) that allowed multiple protective barriers to fail simultaneously.

4. CRITICAL FACTOR 1 — BEHAVIOURAL PATHWAY: DELIBERATE SABOTAGE ACT
4.1 Başlangıç Durumu ve Doğrudan Neden
Derek Walsh, a former employee dismissed 49 days earlier for gross misconduct (repeated safety violations and verbal aggression), deliberately entered the site overnight with intent to cause harm. Using physical access gained through compromised perimeter security and an active access card, Walsh targeted the chlorination unit and manually manipulated safety-critical valves on chlorine storage vessel CV-03, causing an uncontrolled toxic gas release. This was not an accidental failure or procedural error but a premeditated criminal act enabled by multiple organisational security deficiencies.
Derek Walsh deliberately opened the manual emergency vent valve (EV-03B) on chlorine storage vessel CV-03 and disabled the magnetic latch on the automatic pressure relief valve (PRV-03), causing uncontrolled chlorine gas release
4.2 5-Why Analiz Zinciri
NEDEN 1
Why was Walsh able to physically manipulate the CV-03 manual emergency vent valve (EV-03B) and pressure relief valve (PRV-03) magnetic latch?
→ Walsh gained access to Building 3 using his cloned proximity access card which remained active despite his dismissal 49 days earlier, and the CV-03 valves had no tamper-evident seals or anti-tamper mechanical interlocks to prevent unauthorised manual operation
C - Job/Task
NEDEN 2
Why was Walsh's access card still active 49 days after dismissal and why was he able to enter the site undetected?
→ HR department sent email notification to security team on 31 December 2025 but the card was never deactivated; east perimeter CCTV cameras had been offline for 43 days (DVR fault) and the east maintenance gate lock had been broken for 42 days, so Walsh bypassed all physical and electronic security layers undetected
B - Condition
NEDEN 3
Why did security-critical failures (CCTV fault, gate lock failure, access card deactivation) remain unresolved for weeks?
→ Site management treated security-critical maintenance items (CCTV, perimeter gate locks) with the same priority as low-priority general maintenance; no separate escalation pathway existed for security items. Site Manager Angela Collins stated budget constraints were cited and she did not realise the urgency of these items
D - Organisational
NEDEN 4
Why was there no separate prioritisation and escalation mechanism for security-critical maintenance items, and why was no threat assessment conducted for a potentially hostile former employee?
→ The last site security review was conducted 26 months prior with no regular security review schedule in place. Security system gaps (access card deactivation procedure non-enforcement, no overnight access alert, absence of threat assessment policy) had become known deviations tolerated by management
D - Organisational
4.3 Kök Neden
KÖK NEDEN 1: Management Tolerance of Known Deviations in Security Systems
[D1.9 / Organisational] Chemsworth Processing management was aware of multiple security deviations including Walsh's access card remaining active for 49 days post-dismissal, east perimeter CCTV system being offline for 43 days, maintenance gate lock broken for 42 days, and no overnight access alert configured for Building 3, yet tolerated these as low-priority issues. No security review had been conducted for 26 months, no escalation mechanism existed for security-critical maintenance, and no threat assessment policy had been developed for dismissed employees. This systematic tolerance of known security gaps across multiple protective layers created the conditions enabling deliberate sabotage.
4.4 Organizasyonel Faktörler
  • Site security review schedule abandoned — last review 26 months prior to incident, no regular audit programme established
  • Security-critical maintenance items (CCTV, access control, perimeter locks) treated identically to routine low-priority maintenance with no differentiated escalation pathway
  • Access card deactivation procedure existed on paper but had no enforcement mechanism, ownership assignment, or verification process
  • No threat assessment policy for dismissed employees despite Walsh's documented history of safety violations and verbal aggression towards supervisors
5. CRITICAL FACTOR 2 — ACCESS CONTROL FAILURE: ACTIVE CARD 49 DAYS POST-DISMISSAL
5.1 Başlangıç Durumu ve Doğrudan Neden
Derek Walsh's proximity access card, which should have been deactivated within 24 hours of his dismissal on 31 December 2025 per company procedure, remained fully active in the access control system for 49 days. This enabled Walsh to gain legitimate electronic access to Building 3 at 02:00 on 18 February 2026 without triggering any security alert. The access event was logged in the system but no overnight access monitoring or alert mechanism was configured, and Building 3 was unmanned during night shift hours. This critical protective barrier failure was the direct enabler of Walsh's unauthorised entry to the chlorination unit.
Derek Walsh used his access card, still active 49 days after dismissal, to gain unauthorised entry — card deactivation procedure not executed

NEDEN 1

→ Following Walsh's dismissal on 31 December 2025, his access card was not deactivated within the procedurally required 24 hours; the card remained active for 49 days and functioned normally at the Building 3 door at 02:00

→ No automated link existed between the HR system and access control system, and the last security review was 26 months prior. D - Organisational
5.3 Kök Neden
KÖK NEDEN 2: Lack of Accountability for Access Card Deactivation
[D1.3 / Organisational] No clear responsibility assignment or accountability mechanism was defined between HR, security, and management for deactivating access cards of dismissed personnel. The HR Manager sent an email but did not follow up, the security officer did not consider the task assigned to them, and no department was held accountable for the outcome. This ownership vacuum directly enabled Walsh's card to remain active for 49 days and facilitated his unauthorised site entry. The absence of a formal leavers' checklist, sign-off process, and verification mechanism meant this critical security task fell into a procedural gap with no one responsible for ensuring completion.
5.4 Organizasyonel Faktörler
  • Access card deactivation procedure documented but no specific individual or role assigned responsibility for execution and verification
  • Dismissal process relied on informal email communication between HR and security with no formal handover, checklist, or sign-off requirement
  • No automated system integration between HR termination records and access control database to flag or auto-disable cards
  • No periodic audit of active access cards against current employee roster to identify orphaned credentials
6. CRITICAL FACTOR 3 — PHYSICAL SECURITY FAILURE: CCTV OFFLINE 43 DAYS
6.1 Başlangıç Durumu ve Doğrudan Neden
The east perimeter CCTV system, comprising three cameras covering the maintenance access gate area, had been offline for 43 days due to a DVR (Digital Video Recorder) fault logged on 6 January 2026. This fault was recorded in the maintenance system but was not actioned, leaving a critical blind spot in perimeter surveillance. Walsh exploited this gap by entering through the east maintenance gate (which also had a broken lock unrepaired for 42 days) at 01:55 on 18 February, completely undetected. The only operational CCTV footage available was from internal Building 3 cameras, which captured Walsh inside the unit but provided no perimeter breach alert. This prolonged failure of a primary security detection layer was a direct consequence of inadequate maintenance prioritisation and planning.
East perimeter CCTV cameras were offline for 43 days, preventing detection of Walsh's unauthorised site entry

6.3 Kök Neden
KÖK NEDEN 3: Inadequate Maintenance Strategy for Security-Critical Equipment
[D6.1 / Organisational] Chemsworth Processing site had no separate maintenance prioritisation strategy, escalation mechanism, or time-bound repair target defined for security-critical equipment such as the east perimeter CCTV system and perimeter gate locks. These items were queued in the low-priority general maintenance backlog and deferred for weeks citing budget constraints. The last security review having been conducted 26 months prior meant the maintenance strategy was never updated to reflect the security risk profile. Site Manager's characterisation of CCTV repair as 'low-priority' and the 26-month gap in security reviews directly evidences that the maintenance planning strategy did not encompass security-critical assets.
6.4 Organizasyonel Faktörler
  • Maintenance prioritisation system made no distinction between security-critical assets (CCTV, access control, perimeter barriers) and routine operational equipment
  • No time-bound service level agreements or escalation triggers defined for security equipment failures
  • Budget allocation process treated security maintenance as discretionary/deferrable expenditure rather than essential protective infrastructure
  • Security review cycle abandoned — 26-month gap meant maintenance strategy was never aligned with evolving security risk profile or lessons from industry incidents

8. ÇIKARILAN DERSLER
NE YAPILMALI
• Treat access card deactivation as a safety-critical task requiring the same rigour as LOTO or permit-to-work systems — implement automated deactivation, formal verification, and periodic audits to ensure no orphaned credentials exist • Recognise that security-critical equipment (CCTV, access control, perimeter barriers) provides protective barriers equivalent to engineering controls and must have differentiated maintenance prioritisation with time-bound SLAs and escalation mechanisms, not treated as discretionary operational expenditure • Implement threat assessment as a mandatory step in all employee dismissal processes, particularly for cases involving gross misconduct, hostile behaviour, or access to high-hazard areas, with defined mitigation measures and monitoring protocols • Establish multi-departmental accountability for security-critical processes through formal checklists, sign-off requirements, and verification loops — do not rely on informal email communication or assume tasks will be actioned without explicit ownership assignment
UZUN VADELİ ÇÖZÜMLER
• Develop a comprehensive security management system that integrates physical security, access control, threat assessment, and security-critical maintenance into a unified framework with defined roles, responsibilities, performance standards, and audit mechanisms, aligned with industry standards such as ISO 27001 or ASIS International guidelines • Embed security risk assessment into all organisational change processes (employee terminations, facility modifications, process changes, technology implementations) to proactively identify and mitigate emerging vulnerabilities before they can be exploited • Establish a security culture where protective systems are valued as essential infrastructure rather than discretionary overhead, with visible senior leadership commitment, regular communication of security performance, and recognition of security excellence to reinforce desired behaviours • Implement technology solutions (automated HR-access control integration, perimeter intrusion detection, real-time access monitoring, predictive maintenance for security equipment) to reduce reliance on manual processes vulnerable to human error or omission and provide early warning of emerging vulnerabilities
İLETİŞİM VE PAYLAŞIM
• Share lessons learned from this incident across all company sites and with industry peers through anonymised case studies, highlighting the systemic organisational failures that enabled deliberate sabotage and the importance of treating security as a safety-critical function • Conduct briefings for all employees on the incident, emphasising that security is everyone's responsibility, the importance of reporting security concerns (broken locks, non-functional cameras, suspicious behaviour), and the consequences of security complacency • Establish regular security performance communication to all levels of the organisation (monthly dashboard to senior leadership, quarterly updates to all employees) to maintain visibility of security status, celebrate improvements, and reinforce accountability
EĞİTİM VE FARKINDALIK
• Develop and deliver security awareness training for all personnel covering access control procedures, physical security measures, recognition and reporting of security vulnerabilities, insider threat indicators, and individual responsibilities for maintaining site security • Provide specialist training for HR personnel on security implications of employee terminations, threat assessment methodologies, leavers' checklist compliance, and coordination with security and IT departments to ensure comprehensive offboarding • Deliver security-critical maintenance training for facilities and maintenance personnel covering identification of security assets, prioritisation frameworks, SLA requirements, escalation procedures, and the safety consequences of delayed security equipment repairs • Conduct emergency response refresher training for all shift personnel on toxic gas release scenarios, emphasising the importance of immediate response to gas detector alarms, evacuation procedures, and prohibition of alarm dismissal without physical verification, to address the supervisor's initial assumption of false alarm
10. SONUÇ VE ÖNERİLER
10.1 Genel Değerlendirme
This major injury incident resulting from deliberate sabotage by a former employee was enabled by a constellation of systemic organisational failures across multiple security layers. The investigation identified three distinct root causes: management tolerance of known security deviations (D1.9), lack of accountability for access card deactivation (D1.3), and inadequate maintenance strategy for security-critical equipment (D6.1). These root causes are interconnected and mutually reinforcing — the absence of clear accountability allowed critical security tasks to be orphaned, the inadequate maintenance strategy resulted in prolonged equipment failures, and management's tolerance of these known deviations normalised security gaps as acceptable. The incident was not the result of a single procedural lapse or individual error, but rather a systemic degradation of protective barriers over an extended period (26 months since last security review, 49 days with active access card post-dismissal, 43 days with offline CCTV, 42 days with broken perimeter lock). The severity of the outcome — one worker with severe respiratory injury requiring ICU admission and potential permanent lung damage — underscores the life-threatening consequences of security system failures in high-hazard chemical processing environments. The fact that this was a deliberate criminal act does not diminish the organisation's responsibility to maintain robust protective barriers against foreseeable threats, including insider threats from dismissed employees with documented hostile behaviour. The corrective action plan addresses immediate technical fixes (automated card deactivation, CCTV repair, tamper-evident seals) but more importantly establishes the systemic changes required to prevent recurrence: formal accountability frameworks, differentiated security-critical maintenance strategies, threat assessment policies, regular security reviews, and a security culture where protective systems are valued as essential rather than discretionary.
