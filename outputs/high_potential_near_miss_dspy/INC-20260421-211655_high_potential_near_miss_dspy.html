OLAY ÖZETİ

A 2.4 kg torque wrench fell approximately 8 meters from crane pedestal maintenance area, struck scaffold tube, and landed about 1 meter from a rigger. No injuries sustained but high potential for serious harm identified.

1. YÖNETİCİ ÖZETİ
1.1 Olay Özeti
On 08 March 2026 at 06:52, during a scheduled bearing inspection on Crane No. 2 at the pedestal area on Main Deck (+18m), a 3/4 inch drive torque wrench (2.4 kg, 450 mm length, 28 mm diameter handle) was placed on pedestal grating with 40 mm apertures between uses. The wrench shifted and passed through the aperture, falling approximately 8 meters, deflecting from scaffold tube GA-SC-2026-018 before landing on Main Deck approximately 1 meter from rigger Cavid Aliyev. The maintenance task was being performed by Maintenance Technician Rovshan Sultanov (7 years experience) and Apprentice Technician Murad Quliyev (4 months offshore) under valid Permit to Work GA-PTW-2026-0312. Despite a previous similar incident in September 2024 involving a bolt falling through the same grating, the PTW did not identify dropped object risk from grating apertures as a specific hazard.
All work parties stopped immediately upon the incident. The Crane No. 2 pedestal area was isolated and cordoned off. Scaffold GA-SC-2026-018 was suspended and PTW GA-PTW-2026-0312 was cancelled. The scene was preserved and photographed for investigation purposes. Platform OIM was notified at 07:05. All concurrent work tasks on Main Deck were reviewed for similar risks. A comprehensive tool inventory check was completed with all tools accounted for, confirming no additional items were missing or at risk.
1.2 Temel Bulgular
  • Torque wrench handle diameter (28 mm) was smaller than grating aperture (40 mm), creating uncontrolled dropped object hazard
  • PTW GA-PTW-2026-0312 failed to identify dropped object risk from grating apertures despite previous similar incident in September 2024
  • Systematic learning mechanism absent - lessons from previous incident not integrated into PTW templates and hazard identification procedures
  • No organizational accountability defined for reviewing recurring hazards and updating operational control systems

4. CRITICAL FACTOR 1 - DROPPED OBJECT HAZARD CONTROL FAILURE
4.1 Başlangıç Durumu ve Doğrudan Neden
The crane pedestal maintenance area at +18m elevation on Main Deck presented an inherent dropped object hazard due to the presence of grating with 40 mm apertures. This physical configuration, combined with the nature of maintenance work requiring hand tools, created a high-risk environment where small objects could potentially fall through the grating to lower levels where personnel were working. The area below the pedestal included active scaffold work (GA-SC-2026-018) with riggers present, creating a direct line of exposure for personnel to falling objects. Despite a previous similar incident in September 2024 involving a bolt falling through the same grating, no systematic controls had been implemented to prevent recurrence. The work was being conducted under PTW GA-PTW-2026-0312, which did not specifically identify or control the dropped object hazard associated with grating apertures.
Inadequate dropped object hazard control - no controlled drop zone protection or warning systems for objects falling from crane pedestal area
4.2 5-Why Analiz Zinciri
NEDEN 1
Why did the torque wrench pass through the 40 mm grating aperture and fall 8 meters?
→ The torque wrench was placed on the crane pedestal grating between uses during the maintenance task. The wrench handle diameter of 28 mm was smaller than the grating aperture width of 40 mm, creating a 12 mm clearance gap. This dimensional mismatch meant the wrench could not be stably supported by the grating surface. Without proper control or a secure resting point, mechanical movement caused the wrench to shift position, pass through the aperture opening, and fall freely 8 meters to the deck below.
NEDEN 2
Why were workers not trained on safe tool storage in high-risk areas with grating apertures?
→ Workers lacked adequate training and procedural guidance on safe tool storage in high-risk areas, particularly regarding grating with 40 mm apertures. Maintenance Technician Rovshan Sultanov and Apprentice Murad Quliyev had not been trained to identify appropriate interim storage locations during tool use that would prevent dropped object hazards. PTW GA-PTW-2026-0312 did not identify the risk of objects falling through grating apertures as a specific hazard, so workers were not alerted to this risk during pre-job briefing. Even after a similar incident in September 2024 involving a bolt falling through the same grating, this risk was not systematically addressed in subsequent PTW procedures or training programs. The failure resulted from a combination of human factors, procedural deficiencies, and hazard identification system breakdown.
NEDEN 3
Why was the grating aperture dropped object risk not systematically integrated into PTW hazard identification procedures after the September 2024 incident?
→ Following the September 2024 incident, the dropped object risk from grating apertures was not systematically integrated into PTW hazard identification procedures due to multiple system failures. First, the hazard database integration was inadequate - despite the same grating configuration causing problems in both incidents, the specific hazard of small objects falling through 40 mm apertures was not converted into a standard PTW hazard category. Second, the PTW hazard checklist was not updated - as the incident report explicitly states, the PTW for pedestal maintenance did not identify dropped object risk from grating apertures as a specific hazard despite the previous incident. Third, information transfer mechanisms failed - no linkage was established between the September 2024 incident report and the March 2026 PTW procedure, meaning the PTW hazard coordinator or maintenance planner did not reference the previous incident during planning. Fourth, corrective action monitoring failed - the hazard identification procedure lacked periodic review mechanisms to systematically integrate lessons learned from previous incidents into new PTW templates. The result was that 17+ months elapsed between incidents without the same physical condition and risk mechanism being addressed.
NEDEN 4
Why does the PTW hazard identification procedure lack periodic review and feedback mechanisms to systematically integrate specific hazard information from previous similar incidents into new PTW templates?
→ The PTW hazard identification procedure lacks periodic review and feedback mechanisms to systematically integrate specific hazard information from previous incidents into new PTW templates, resulting in the grating aperture dropped object risk not being identified in the March 2026 PTW despite the September 2024 incident. This represents a fundamental failure of the risk management system to learn from historical incidents and translate those learnings into procedural controls. The absence of periodic review processes, post-incident feedback loops, and template update mechanisms prevents the identification of recurring hazards in specific work environments. Without these systematic mechanisms, the same hazards repeatedly manifest because there is no organizational process to capture, codify, and embed lessons learned into operational procedures.
NEDEN 5
Why has the organization not integrated proactive review of recurring hazards and post-incident template update procedures into the PTW hazard identification system at the corporate level?
→ At the organizational level, the PTW hazard identification system lacks integration of proactive review mechanisms for recurring hazards and post-incident template update procedures due to fundamental system design failures. First, the learning loop has failed - the September 2024 bolt drop incident was not standardized as a recognized hazard category for small tools falling through 40 mm grating apertures, allowing the same hazard to recur 18 months later without any mechanism to automatically incorporate lessons learned into PTW templates. Second, systemic control is absent - PTW-GA-PTW-2026-0312 did not list dropped objects as a specific hazard despite the same pedestal area and grating configuration, indicating change management or Management of Change procedures failed to transfer information from previous incidents to new permit templates. Third, accountability gaps exist - it is undefined who is responsible for reviewing hazards derived from previous incidents at what intervals and updating PTW templates accordingly, making recurring hazards nobody's responsibility. The organizational result is that the incident analysis to learning extraction to PTW template update to validation cycle has not been designed or implemented at the corporate level, causing similar hazards to systematically recur.
4.3 Kök Neden (Organizational Learning System Failure - Incident Lessons Not Systematically Integrated into PTW Templates)
KÖK NEDEN 1: Organizational Learning System Failure - Incident Lessons Not Systematically Integrated into PTW Templates
The fundamental root cause is the absence of a systematic organizational learning mechanism at the corporate level to capture lessons from previous incidents and integrate them into operational control systems, specifically PTW templates and hazard identification procedures. The September 2024 bolt drop incident through the same 40 mm grating apertures should have triggered a systematic update to PTW templates for pedestal maintenance work, creating a standardized hazard category for small objects falling through grating apertures. However, 18 months later, the March 2026 PTW-GA-PTW-2026-0312 did not identify this specific hazard, demonstrating that no mechanism exists to automatically incorporate incident-derived lessons into permit templates. This organizational learning failure is compounded by undefined accountability - no roles or responsibilities are established for who reviews incident-derived hazards at what intervals and ensures PTW templates are updated accordingly. The result is that recurring hazards become nobody's responsibility, and the organization systematically fails to prevent similar incidents despite having clear historical evidence of the risk. This represents a fundamental breakdown in the incident analysis to learning extraction to PTW template update to validation cycle at the organizational management system level.

5. CRITICAL FACTOR 2 - IMPROPER TOOL PLACEMENT AND STORAGE
5.1 Başlangıç Durumu ve Doğrudan Neden
During the crane pedestal bearing inspection maintenance task, the maintenance team required the use of a 3/4 inch drive torque wrench as part of their standard tool kit. The work was being conducted on pedestal grating with 40 mm apertures, a physical configuration that presented an inherent risk for small objects to fall through to lower levels. The torque wrench had a handle diameter of 28 mm, which was 12 mm smaller than the grating aperture width. Between uses during the maintenance task, the technician placed the wrench directly on the grating surface rather than using a secure storage method such as a tool tray, tool bag, or designated storage area. This placement decision was made without recognition of the dimensional mismatch between the tool and the grating aperture, and without awareness of the dropped object hazard this created. The work was being conducted under PTW GA-PTW-2026-0312, which did not provide specific guidance on tool storage or control measures to prevent dropped objects in areas with grating apertures.
Improper tool placement - torque wrench with 28 mm diameter placed on grating with 40 mm apertures, creating uncontrolled dropped object hazard
5.2 5-Why Analiz Zinciri
NEDEN 1
Why did the 28 mm diameter torque wrench become unstable and fall through the 40 mm grating aperture?
→ When the torque wrench with its 28 mm diameter body was placed on the grating with 40 mm apertures, the dimensional mismatch prevented stable support. The 12 mm clearance gap between the wrench diameter and the aperture width meant the wrench could not achieve stable positioning on the grating surface and was subject to gravitational forces causing it to shift and fall. The wrench lost its support surface, passed through the void, and fell freely 8 meters to the deck below. The physical impossibility of controlling an object when its dimensions are smaller than the supporting surface apertures made the dropped object incident inevitable once the wrench was placed on the grating.
NEDEN 2
Why was the placement of the torque wrench on 40 mm aperture grating not identified and controlled as a dropped object risk in the valid PTW and maintenance procedures?
→ The placement of the torque wrench on 40 mm aperture pedestal grating was not identified and controlled as a dropped object risk due to multiple systematic failures. First, hazard identification failed - when preparing PTW GA-PTW-2026-0312, the incompatibility between grating aperture dimensions and torque wrench diameter was not systematically evaluated, and dropped object risk was not listed as a specific hazard in the PTW template. Second, lessons from the previous incident were not learned - despite a bolt falling through the same grating in September 2024, this information was not adequately integrated into the PTW preparation procedure or risk register, and the recurring hazard was not recognized. Third, work control system implementation was deficient - the maintenance procedure should have specified dropped object risk assessment as a valid control point, but this control was either not applied or was bypassed by the PTW preparation team. Fourth, control measures were absent - no mitigation controls were added to the PTW prohibiting direct placement of tools on grating or requiring alternative storage methods. Fifth, management oversight was inadequate - HSE expertise was not sufficiently involved in PTW approval to verify dropped object risk controls before work authorization.
NEDEN 3
Why was a recurring hazard procedure or technical control criteria for grating apertures not integrated into the system after the September 2024 bolt drop incident?
→ Following the September 2024 bolt drop incident, a recurring hazard procedure or technical control criteria for grating apertures was not integrated into the system due to multiple organizational failures. First, inter-incident learning was absent - after the September 2024 incident, no updates were made to the platform HSE system for dropped object risk from the same grating, and the consequences of the previous incident were not reflected in PTW checklists or technical procedures. Second, hazard identification was deficient - the 40 mm grating apertures, which are larger than the 28 mm torque wrench handle, were not identified as a potential dropped object source, and no specific control criteria were established for this grating configuration. Third, recurring hazard protocols were missing - no systematic procedure or technical control rule was established for the recurring nature of dropped object risk at the same location. Fourth, PTW preparation standards were inadequate - no written control procedure or criteria exists in the PTW preparation process requiring comparison of grating aperture dimensions with hand tool dimensions. Fifth, management control was weak - after the September 2024 incident, the platform HSE department did not ensure integration of risks categorized as recurring hazards into the system.
NEDEN 4
Why were technical control criteria and PTW checklist updates for similar grating aperture hazards not systematically implemented in the platform HSE system after the September 2024 dropped object incident?
→ The systematic implementation of technical control criteria and PTW checklist updates for grating aperture hazards failed after the September 2024 incident due to two fundamental system failures. First, the post-incident learning and corrective control mechanism did not function - during the 18-month period between the September 2024 incident and the March 2026 incident, the hazard assessment related to the same 40 mm grating aperture was not updated and this information was not communicated to PTW preparation personnel, representing a system control deficiency in hazard management. Second, recurring hazard information was not integrated into the PTW system and control criteria - no formal mechanism exists to add risk control criteria derived from previous incidents to PTW checklists and convert them into operational procedures, representing failures in procedural adequacy and operational guidelines. The result is that the platform has not established a systematic control mechanism for known recurring hazards, indicating a deficiency in organizational memory and cross-functional information sharing.
NEDEN 5
Why has organizational responsibility and management mechanism for systematically integrating lessons from recurring hazards into PTW and operational guidelines not been defined in platform operations management and company group standards?
→ In platform operations management, organizational responsibility and management mechanisms for systematic integration of lessons from recurring hazards have not been defined due to multiple fundamental failures. First, the risk assessment cycle is broken - after the September 2024 dropped object incident, specific control measures for grating aperture risk were not defined, and the March 2026 PTW did not recognize this risk again, meaning lessons from the first incident were not applied. Second, PTW system design is deficient - the PTW template is not designed to automatically recognize grating aperture risk in pedestal maintenance tasks, representing a system-level design gap. Third, systematic compliance monitoring is absent - no audit or compliance tracking system exists to verify whether controls against grating risk are being implemented. Fourth, organizational learning culture is weak at the corporate level - after the first incident, no responsible management role or mechanism was assigned for systematic integration into operational guidelines. Fifth, recurring failure analysis is incomplete - when the same failure recurred 18 months later, responsibility for not implementing the root cause analysis results from the first incident was not determined.

8. ÇIKARILAN DERSLER
NE YAPILMALI
• Always verify dimensional compatibility between tools and work surfaces - before placing any tool or equipment on grating, scaffolding, or other surfaces with apertures, verify that the tool dimensions are larger than the aperture dimensions to prevent dropped object hazards. Implement mandatory dimensional verification as part of pre-job planning and hazard assessment for all work at height. • Systematically integrate lessons from previous incidents into operational procedures - establish formal mechanisms to review all incident reports, extract specific lessons learned, and update relevant PTW templates, procedures, and training materials within defined timelines. Assign clear accountability for this integration and verify implementation through audit and compliance monitoring. • Implement multiple layers of dropped object prevention controls - use a hierarchy of controls including engineering controls (grating covers, barriers), administrative controls (PTW requirements, exclusion zones), and personal protective equipment (tool lanyards, tethering systems) to prevent dropped objects. Never rely on a single control measure for high-consequence hazards. • Establish systematic compliance monitoring for incident-derived controls - after implementing corrective actions from incident investigations, establish ongoing monitoring and audit programs to verify that controls remain effective over time. Include incident-derived control verification as a standard element of routine safety audits and management reviews.
UZUN VADELİ ÇÖZÜMLER
• Design and implement comprehensive organizational learning management system - develop corporate-level systems and procedures that systematically capture lessons from incidents, integrate them into operational controls, define clear accountability for implementation, and verify effectiveness through ongoing monitoring. This system should operate as a continuous improvement cycle embedded in the organization's management processes. • Enhance PTW system with automated hazard recognition capabilities - invest in PTW system technology that automatically flags known recurring hazards based on work location, equipment configuration, and task type. Build organizational knowledge into the system design so that lessons learned are systematically applied to future work planning without relying solely on individual memory or competence. • Develop mature organizational safety culture focused on learning and improvement - implement long-term culture change initiatives that prioritize learning from incidents, encourage proactive hazard identification and reporting, and recognize individuals and teams who contribute to organizational learning. Build a culture where recurring incidents are seen as organizational system failures rather than individual errors. • Establish robust management system governance and accountability - implement senior management accountability frameworks that include regular review of organizational learning system effectiveness, incident lesson integration metrics, and recurring hazard elimination progress. Make organizational learning system performance a key element of management performance evaluation and organizational strategic planning.
İLETİŞİM VE PAYLAŞIM
• Immediate platform-wide safety alert - issue comprehensive safety alert to all personnel describing both the September 2024 and March 2026 incidents, highlighting the recurring nature of the hazard, and mandating immediate implementation of dropped object prevention controls for all work at height above grating areas. • Cross-platform and corporate-wide lesson sharing - communicate incident details, root cause analysis findings, and corrective actions to all company platforms and facilities to prevent similar incidents across the organization. Include specific technical details about grating aperture dimensions and tool compatibility requirements. • Regular organizational learning effectiveness updates - establish routine communication channels to share information about incident lesson integration progress, corrective action implementation status, and organizational learning system performance metrics with all levels of the organization from frontline workers to senior management.
EĞİTİM VE FARKINDALIK
• Mandatory dropped object hazard awareness training - develop and deliver comprehensive training program for all personnel working at height or below work-at-height areas, covering hazard recognition, dimensional compatibility verification, tool storage and control requirements, and use of dropped object prevention equipment including tool lanyards and tethering systems. • PTW preparer and approver competency enhancement - provide specialized training for all personnel involved in PTW preparation and approval, focusing on systematic hazard identification techniques, integration of lessons learned from previous incidents, and verification of adequate control measures before work authorization. Include practical exercises using real incident case studies. • Supervisor and leadership training on organizational learning - develop training program for supervisors and managers on their roles and responsibilities in the organizational learning system, including incident lesson extraction, procedural updating, implementation verification, and ongoing compliance monitoring. Emphasize leadership accountability for learning system effectiveness. • Recurring hazard recognition and control training - provide specialized training on identifying patterns of recurring hazards, understanding root causes of recurrence, and implementing systematic controls to prevent repetition. Include training on how to use incident databases and learning management systems to access and apply historical lessons learned.

10.1 Genel Değerlendirme
This investigation has identified fundamental organizational system failures that extend far beyond the immediate circumstances of the dropped object near-miss incident. While the immediate cause was the placement of a torque wrench on grating with apertures larger than the tool diameter, the root causes reveal systematic weaknesses in how the organization learns from previous incidents and translates those lessons into effective operational controls. The recurrence of essentially the same incident 18 months after a previous similar event demonstrates that the organization lacks the systematic mechanisms necessary to prevent recurring hazards despite having clear historical evidence of specific risks. The investigation identified two interconnected root causes that converge on a common meta root cause: the absence of a designed and implemented organizational learning system at the corporate level. This meta root cause represents a comprehensive failure in the organization's ability to systematically extract knowledge from incidents, integrate that knowledge into operational control systems, define clear accountability for implementation, and verify through monitoring and audit that integration has been effective. The failure manifests across multiple organizational functions including incident investigation, PTW system design, hazard identification procedures, change management processes, and compliance monitoring systems. The organizational impact extends beyond this specific incident to affect all operational risk management processes. The weak organizational learning culture, combined with absent systematic compliance monitoring, means that lessons identified from any incident may not be translated into sustained operational improvements. This creates a pattern where the organization repeatedly experiences similar incidents without developing institutional memory or implementing effective preventive measures. The investigation findings indicate that the organization operates at a reactive rather than proactive safety management maturity level, lacking the systematic learning and improvement capabilities fundamental to high-reliability operations. The corrective action plan addresses both immediate hazard controls and long-term organizational system improvements. Urgent actions focus on implementing dropped object prevention controls and revising PTW templates to prevent recurrence of this specific hazard. High-priority actions establish the systematic incident learning integration procedures and accountability structures necessary to prevent similar organizational learning failures in the future. Medium and long-term actions focus on enhancing PTW system capabilities, implementing compliance monitoring programs, and developing the organizational culture and management system governance necessary for sustained safety performance improvement. Successful implementation of this comprehensive corrective action plan will require sustained senior management commitment, adequate resource allocation, and ongoing verification of effectiveness through systematic monitoring and audit.
