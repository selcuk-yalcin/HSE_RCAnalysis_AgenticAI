Olay Tipi
High Potential Near Miss - Dropped Object

OLAY ÖZETİ

A 2.4 kg torque wrench fell 8 meters from a crane pedestal work area, striking a scaffold tube and landing near a rigger. The wrench passed through a grating aperture larger than its handle, highlighting a recurring dropped object risk not addressed in the Permit to Work.

1. YÖNETİCİ ÖZETİ
1.1 Olay Özeti
On March 8, 2026, at approximately 06:52, a maintenance team consisting of Rovshan Sultanov and Murad Quliyev was performing a scheduled bearing inspection on Crane No. 2 at the pedestal area on the Main Deck of the Gunashli Alpha Platform. During this task, a 2.4 kg torque wrench, approximately 450 mm in length and 28 mm in diameter, was temporarily placed on the pedestal grating. The grating had apertures of 40 mm, which were larger than the wrench's handle diameter. The wrench shifted, fell through an aperture, and dropped approximately 8 meters, deflecting off a scaffold tube before landing about 1 meter from rigger Cavid Aliyev. Fortunately, no injuries were sustained, but the incident had a high potential for serious harm.
Immediately following the incident, all work parties in the area, including the pedestal team and scaffold riggers, ceased operations. The Crane No. 2 pedestal area was promptly isolated and cordoned off, and Scaffold GA-SC-2026-018 was suspended. The active Permit to Work (PTW No. GA-PTW-2026-0312) was cancelled, and the scene was preserved and photographed for investigation. The Platform OIM was notified at 07:05, and a review of all concurrent work tasks on the Main Deck was initiated. A comprehensive tool inventory check was also completed, confirming all tools were accounted for.
1.2 Temel Bulgular
  • Torque wrench fell through a grating aperture larger than its handle diameter.
  • Permit to Work (PTW) did not identify this specific dropped object risk.
  • A similar dropped object incident occurred in September 2024, but lessons were not integrated.
  • Lack of systematic learning from incidents and PTW template updates.

3. ANALİZ YÖNTEMİ - 5 WHY
3.1 5-Why Tekniği
The 5-Why technique was applied to each identified immediate cause. For each immediate cause, a series of 'Why' questions were posed, with each answer forming the basis for the next 'Why' question. This iterative process continued for at least five levels or until a root cause, typically an organizational or systemic failure, was identified. The aim was to drill down from the observable symptoms to the core reasons that allowed the incident to occur, ensuring that corrective actions target the true source of the problem rather than just its manifestations.

4. CRITICAL FACTOR 1 - INADEQUATE DROPPED OBJECT PROTECTION
4.1 Başlangıç Durumu ve Doğrudan Neden
A 2.4 kg torque wrench fell 8 meters from the crane pedestal work area, striking a scaffold tube and landing near a rigger. This occurred because the controlled drop zone protection and warning systems for falling objects from the crane pedestal area (+18m) were insufficient. There was a lack of protection from the impact of falling objects in the open areas of the scaffold and deck below.
Inadequate controlled drop zone protection and warning systems for falling objects from the crane pedestal area.
4.2 5-Why Analiz Zinciri
NEDEN 1
Why did the torque wrench fall 8 meters through the 40 mm aperture of the pedestal grating?
→ The torque wrench was placed on the crane pedestal grating between uses. Since the wrench's 28 mm diameter was smaller than the 40 mm width of the grating apertures, the wrench was prone to sliding and shifting on the grating. The wrench was not kept under control or provided with a stable resting point, leading to its mechanical movement through the aperture and an 8-meter fall.
NEDEN 2
Why were the workers' tool handling procedures and safety training inadequate regarding tool storage in high-risk areas with grating apertures?
→ The inability to keep the wrench under control stemmed from insufficient training and procedures for workers on safe tool storage in high-risk areas, particularly those with 40mm grating apertures. Maintenance Technician Rovshan Sultanov and Apprentice Murad Quliyev were not trained to identify suitable temporary storage locations for tools during use. The PTW GA-PTW-2026-0312 did not identify the risk of falling objects through grating apertures as a specific hazard, leading workers to place the wrench on the grating without considering this risk. Even after a similar incident in September 2024, this risk was not systematically addressed or reiterated in subsequent PTWs. The failure is a combination of human factors, procedural deficiencies, and hazard identification failures.
NEDEN 3
Why was the risk of falling objects through grating apertures not systematically included in the PTW hazard identification procedure after a similar incident in September 2024, and why was this information not transferred to subsequent maintenance tasks?
→ The risk of falling objects through grating apertures was not systematically included in the PTW hazard identification procedure after the similar incident in September 2024 due to: 1. Lack of Hazard Database Integration: Despite the previous incident (a bolt falling) and the March 2026 incident involving the same grating (40 mm aperture), this hazard was not standardized as a PTW hazard category for "objects falling through grating apertures." 2. Failure to Update PTW Hazard Checklist: The report states that "PTW for pedestal maintenance did not identify dropped object risk from grating apertures as a specific hazard, despite a previous dropped object incident (September 2024, bolt through same grating)." This clear deficiency indicates that it was not added to PTW templates/checklists as a remedial action after the September 2024 incident. 3. Lack of Information Transfer Mechanism: There was no information link established between the September 2024 incident report and the March 2026 PTW procedure. The PTW hazard coordinator or maintenance planner did not use a control mechanism that referenced previous incidents. 4. Failure to Monitor Corrective Actions: The D4.1 hazard identification procedure likely does not include a periodic review to systematically integrate specific hazards learned from previous incidents into new PTWs. Result: Despite over 17 months between the two incidents, the same physical condition (grating aperture) and the same risk mechanism (small tools falling) recurred.
NEDEN 4
Why does the PTW hazard identification procedure lack a cyclical review and feedback mechanism to systematically integrate specific hazard information from similar previous incidents into new PTW templates?
→ The PTW hazard identification procedure lacks a cyclical review and feedback mechanism to systematically integrate specific hazard information obtained from similar previous incidents (such as the September 2024 dropped bolt incident) into new PTW templates. Consequently, the risk of a torque wrench falling through grating apertures (40 mm holes) was not identified and was replicated in the March 2026 PTW. This demonstrates the failure of the risk management system to learn from historical accidents and translate these learnings into procedures. The absence of periodic review, post-incident feedback, and template update mechanisms prevents the identification of recurring hazards in specific work environments.
NEDEN 5
Why is the proactive review for recurring hazards and the post-incident template update procedure not integrated or implemented at a corporate level in the design of the PTW hazard identification system?
→ The proactive review for recurring hazards and the post-incident template update procedure are not integrated at a corporate level within the PTW hazard identification system because: 1. Failed Learning Cycle: The bolt dropping incident in September 2024 was not standardized as a "risk of small tools falling through 40 mm aperture gratings." This hazard recurred 18 months after the previous incident. There is no mechanism within the organization to automatically incorporate lessons extracted from previous incidents into PTW templates. 2. Systemic Control Deficiency: PTW-GA-PTW-2026-0312 did not list dropped objects as a specific hazard, despite the same grating apertures being present in the same pedestal area. This indicates a failure in change management or MoC procedures – information from previous incidents was not transferred to new permit templates. 3. Accountability Gap: It is not defined who reviews hazards derived from previous incidents and updates PTW templates, or at what intervals. Consequently, recurring hazards have become "no one's responsibility." Result: At the corporate level, the incident analysis → learning extraction → PTW template update → validation cycle is either not designed or not implemented. As a result, similar hazards systematically recur.
4.3 Kök Neden (Failure in Corporate Learning Cycle and PTW System Integration)
KÖK NEDEN 1: Failure in Corporate Learning Cycle and PTW System Integration
The organization failed to systematically integrate lessons learned from previous incidents into its Permit to Work (PTW) hazard identification system. This includes a lack of proactive review for recurring hazards, ineffective change management processes for updating PTW templates, and an absence of clear accountability for ensuring that incident-derived information is incorporated into operational controls. This systemic weakness allowed a known hazard to recur, demonstrating a breakdown in the continuous improvement loop.
4.4 Organizasyonel Faktörler
  • Lack of a systematic mechanism to integrate lessons learned from previous incidents into PTW templates.
  • Ineffective change management (MoC) process for updating PTW templates with new hazard information.
  • Absence of clear accountability for reviewing and updating PTW templates based on incident analysis.
  • Failure to establish a robust incident analysis → learning extraction → PTW template update → validation cycle at the corporate level.
5. CRITICAL FACTOR 2 - IMPROPER TOOL PLACEMENT AND STORAGE
5.1 Başlangıç Durumu ve Doğrudan Neden
A torque wrench with a 28mm diameter was temporarily placed on a grating with 40mm apertures. The wrench was of a size that could fall through the grating aperture, and this risk was not identified when the tool was stored.
Improper positioning/storage of tools/equipment, specifically placing a torque wrench on a grating with larger apertures.
5.2 5-Why Analiz Zinciri
NEDEN 1
Why did the 28 mm diameter torque wrench become susceptible to falling through when placed on a 40 mm aperture grating?
→ When the torque wrench, with its 28 mm diameter body, was placed on the 40 mm aperture grating, its stability could not be maintained because the wrench's diameter was smaller than the grating holes. Under the influence of gravity, the wrench was prone to sliding and falling. The 12 mm difference between the wrench's diameter (28 mm) and the grating aperture (40 mm) made it impossible to keep the object under control. The wrench lost its supporting surface, fell into the void, and free-fell 8 meters.
NEDEN 2
Why was the placement of the torque wrench on the 40 mm aperture pedestal grating not identified and controlled as a dropped object risk in the Permit to Work (PTW No. GA-PTW-2026-0312) and maintenance procedures?
→ The placement of the torque wrench on the 40 mm aperture pedestal grating was not identified and controlled as a dropped object risk because: 1. Hazard Identification Failure: During the preparation of PTW GA-PTW-2026-0312, the incompatibility between the grating aperture dimensions (40 mm) and the torque wrench diameter (28 mm), and the potential falling risk, were not systematically evaluated. The dropped object risk was not listed as a specific hazard in the PTW template. 2. No Lessons Learned from Previous Incident: Despite a bolt dropping incident from the same grating in September 2024, this information was not sufficiently integrated into the PTW preparation procedure or risk register. The recurring hazard was not recognized. 3. Lack of D4.1 Work Control System Implementation: The maintenance procedure should have specified a dropped object risk assessment as a valid control point, but this control was either not applied or appears to have been bypassed by the PTW preparation team. 4. Lack of Control Measures: The PTW did not include mitigation controls prohibiting the direct placement of tools on the grating or requiring alternative storage methods. 5. Management Gap: There was an absence or inadequacy of HSE expertise involvement to check for dropped object risks before PTW approval.
NEDEN 3
Why was a recurring hazard procedure for dropped object risk or technical control criteria related to grating apertures not integrated into the system when PTW GA-PTW-2026-0312 was prepared, especially after the bolt falling incident from the same grating in September 2024?
→ A recurring hazard procedure for dropped object risk or technical control criteria related to grating apertures was not integrated into the system when PTW GA-PTW-2026-0312 was prepared because: 1. Lack of Learning Between Incidents: After the bolt dropping incident in September 2024, no update was made in the platform HSE system for the dropped object risk originating from the same grating; the results of the previous incident were not reflected in the PTW checklist or technical procedures. 2. Hazard Identification Deficiency: The 40 mm grating apertures (larger than the 28 mm torque wrench handle) were not identified as a potential dropped object source; specific control criteria for this grating configuration were not established. 3. Absence of Recurring Hazard Protocol: A systematic procedure or technical control rule for dropped object risk of a recurring nature in the same location (Crane Pedestal, grating area) was not established. 4. Inadequacy of PTW Preparation Standards: There is no criterion or written control procedure in the PTW preparation process that requires comparing grating aperture dimensions with hand tool dimensions. 5. Weak Management Control: After the September 2024 incident, the integration of risks categorized as "recurring hazards" by the platform HSE department was not ensured.
NEDEN 4
Why were systematic implementation of technical control criteria and PTW checklist updates for similar grating aperture hazards not ensured in the platform HSE system after the September 2024 dropped object incident, and why was this "recurring hazard" information not communicated to PTW preparers and control authorities?
→ The systematic implementation of technical control criteria and PTW checklist updates for similar grating aperture hazards in the platform HSE system after the September 2024 dropped object incident was not ensured due to two fundamental system failures: Firstly: The post-incident learning and corrective control mechanism did not function. In the 18 months between the similar incident in September 2024 (screw falling through grating) and the March 2026 incident (torque wrench falling), the hazard assessment related to the same grating aperture (40mm) was not updated, and this information was not communicated to the personnel preparing the PTW. This represents a lack of system control under D1.3 (Control Adequacy - Hazard Management). Secondly: The "recurring hazard" information was not integrated into the PTW system and control criteria. There is no formal mechanism for adding risk control criteria derived from previous incidents to the PTW checklist and converting them into operating procedures. This represents a failure of D9.1 (Procedure/Checklist Adequacy) and D3.2 (Operational Guidelines). Result: The platform has not established a systematic control mechanism for "known recurring hazards." This indicates a lack of corporate memory and cross-functional information sharing.
NEDEN 5
Why is the corporate responsibility and management mechanism for systematically integrating lessons learned from recurring hazards into PTW and operational guidelines not defined within platform operations management and company group standards?
→ In platform operations management, the corporate responsibility and management mechanism for the systematic integration of lessons learned from recurring hazards (objects falling through grating apertures) is not defined. The reasons for this are: 1. Disconnected Risk Assessment Cycle: After the September 2024 dropped object incident, specific control measures for the grating aperture risk were not defined. Since the March 2026 PTW did not re-identify this risk, lessons from the first incident were not applied. 2. Deficiency in PTW System Design: The PTW template is not designed to automatically recognize the grating aperture risk in pedestal maintenance tasks. This is a system-level design gap under D4.4 (PTW System Failure). 3. Absence of Systematic Compliance Monitoring: There is no audit or compliance tracking system to verify whether controls against grating risk are being implemented (D9.5 - Inadequate Monitoring/Audit). 4. Weak Learning Culture at Corporate Level: After the first incident, a responsible management role or mechanism for systematic integration into operational guidelines was not assigned (D1.7 - Weak Learning Culture). 5. Lack of Recurring Failure Analysis: When the same failure recurred 18 months later, accountability for not applying the root cause analysis results of the first incident was not determined.
5.3 Kök Neden (Systemic Failure in Learning Integration and Risk Management Oversight)
KÖK NEDEN 2: Systemic Failure in Learning Integration and Risk Management Oversight
The platform's operational management lacks a defined corporate responsibility and management mechanism for the systematic integration of lessons learned from recurring hazards into PTW and operational guidelines. This includes a disconnected risk assessment cycle, design deficiencies in the PTW system, absence of systematic compliance monitoring, a weak corporate learning culture, and a failure to analyze recurring failures to assign accountability for non-application of previous lessons.
5.4 Organizasyonel Faktörler
  • Disconnected risk assessment cycle where lessons from previous incidents are not integrated into new PTWs.
  • PTW system design does not automatically recognize recurring hazards for specific work areas.
  • Absence of systematic monitoring and audit mechanisms to ensure compliance with risk controls.
  • Weak corporate learning culture that fails to systematically integrate lessons learned into procedures.
  • Lack of accountability for non-application of root cause analysis results from recurring incidents.

8. ÇIKARILAN DERSLER
NE YAPILMALI
• Always ensure that all potential dropped object hazards, especially those identified in previous incidents, are explicitly addressed and controlled in the Permit to Work (PTW) and associated risk assessments. This includes considering the size of tools relative to grating apertures. • Implement a robust system for capturing, analyzing, and integrating lessons learned from all incidents and near misses into operational procedures, training programs, and PTW templates. This system must ensure that information from recurring hazards is systematically applied. • Provide comprehensive and recurring training to all personnel involved in work at height on proper tool handling, temporary storage, and dropped object prevention techniques, emphasizing the risks associated with grating and open areas. • Establish clear accountability and a defined process for the periodic review and update of safety documentation, including PTWs and risk registers, to reflect new learnings and evolving risks.
UZUN VADELİ ÇÖZÜMLER
• Develop and embed a proactive safety culture where continuous improvement from incident learning is a core value, driven by leadership commitment and active employee participation. This includes fostering an environment where hazards are reported without fear of reprisal. • Invest in engineering controls and design improvements, such as smaller mesh grating or secondary containment, in areas prone to dropped objects, especially where work at height is frequent. • Implement advanced digital PTW systems that can automatically flag recurring hazards based on location, equipment, and task history, ensuring that past lessons are not overlooked. • Establish a robust audit and assurance program that regularly verifies the effectiveness of safety management systems, including the integration of lessons learned and the application of risk controls across all operations.
İLETİŞİM VE PAYLAŞIM
• Create a formal communication protocol to disseminate lessons learned from incidents across all relevant departments and shifts, ensuring that all personnel are aware of recurring hazards and updated control measures. • Utilize safety briefings, toolbox talks, and digital platforms to regularly reinforce the importance of dropped object prevention and the specific risks identified in this incident. • Encourage open dialogue and feedback channels for workers to report potential hazards and suggest improvements to safety procedures, ensuring that their practical experience informs safety management.
EĞİTİM VE FARKINDALIK
• Develop specialized training modules on dropped object prevention for personnel working at height, covering topics such as tool tethering, secure storage, and hazard identification specific to grating and open structures. • Integrate case studies from actual incidents, including this one and the September 2024 event, into safety training to highlight the real-world consequences of procedural failures and the importance of vigilance. • Implement refresher training at regular intervals to ensure that knowledge of dropped object prevention techniques and updated procedures remains current and effective. • Provide specific training for PTW preparers and approvers on how to effectively identify and mitigate dropped object risks, particularly those that are recurring or location-specific, and how to utilize incident databases for hazard identification.
10. SONUÇ VE ÖNERİLER
10.1 Genel Değerlendirme
This incident, a high-potential near miss involving a dropped torque wrench, underscores significant systemic weaknesses within the organization's safety management system, particularly concerning learning from past incidents and the effectiveness of its Permit to Work (PTW) process. The recurrence of a dropped object hazard from the same grating aperture, despite a similar event 18 months prior, highlights a critical failure in the corporate learning cycle. There is a clear disconnect between incident investigation findings and their integration into operational controls, leading to a persistent vulnerability to known risks. The absence of systematic updates to PTW templates, inadequate training on tool handling in high-risk areas, and a lack of clear accountability for ensuring that lessons learned are actioned, collectively contributed to this preventable incident. Addressing these root causes requires a fundamental shift in how the organization manages safety information, implements corrective actions, and fosters a culture of continuous improvement.
10.2 Kısa Vadeli Önlemler (1-2 Ay)
  • Immediately update all PTW templates for elevated work areas, especially those with grating, to explicitly include dropped object hazards and mandatory control measures like tool tethering or secondary containment.
  • Conduct urgent refresher training for all personnel involved in work at height on dropped object prevention, focusing on secure tool storage and the risks associated with grating apertures.
  • Implement a temporary 'no tools on grating' policy for all elevated work areas until permanent engineering controls or robust procedural changes are fully implemented and verified.
  • Establish a rapid review process for all active PTWs to ensure that dropped object risks are adequately identified and controlled, with immediate suspension of work if deficiencies are found.
10.3 Uzun Vadeli İyileştirmeler (3-12 Ay)
  • Design and implement a comprehensive, integrated system for incident learning that ensures all lessons learned from investigations are systematically captured, analyzed, and translated into updates for PTW templates, risk assessments, and operational procedures.
  • Develop a robust change management (MoC) process specifically for safety-related updates, ensuring that all modifications to procedures, equipment, or work environments are thoroughly assessed for new or recurring hazards.
  • Strengthen the safety culture through leadership commitment, visible engagement, and empowering employees to stop unsafe work and report hazards without fear of reprisal, fostering a proactive approach to safety.
  • Invest in engineering solutions, such as installing smaller mesh grating or solid flooring where feasible, and providing dedicated, secure tool storage solutions for all elevated work areas to eliminate or minimize dropped object risks at the source.
